INVESTMENT GUIDE

Investment basics

Investing means putting money into an asset with the hope that it may grow in value or generate income. Returns are never guaranteed, and you can lose some or all of the money invested.

1. Common types of investments

Different investments work in different ways. Examples include:

  • Stocks: Shares of ownership in a company. Their prices can rise or fall.
  • Bonds: Loans made to a government or company that may pay interest. The issuer may fail to make payments.
  • Mutual funds: Pooled money invested in a collection of assets and managed according to the fund\u2019s strategy. Fees and risks vary.
  • Real estate: Property bought in the hope of earning rental income or selling later at a higher price. Property can be difficult to sell quickly and has costs.

2. Risk and reward

Risk is the possibility that an investment will perform worse than expected or lose value. Higher potential returns often come with higher risk.

3. Diversification

Diversification means spreading money across different investments rather than relying on one. It can reduce the impact of a single investment performing poorly, but it cannot eliminate risk.

4. Time horizon

Your time horizon is how long you expect to keep money invested. Money needed soon is generally not suitable for volatile investments.

5. Long-term investing

Long-term investing means staying invested over an extended period in line with your goals and risk tolerance. Regular saving and patience may help, but markets can fall and losses are possible. Review your plan when your circumstances or goals change.

6. Fees and research

Understand fees, liquidity, who operates a product, and how it is regulated before making a decision. Be cautious of pressure to act quickly or promises of guaranteed high returns.

Before you invest

  • Set a clear goal and understand when you may need the money.
  • Keep emergency savings separate from money you invest.
  • Check the provider, product documents, fees and applicable regulation.
  • Never invest because someone pressures you or promises guaranteed high returns.
  • Do not invest money you cannot afford to lose.
Remember: This information is general in nature, not a recommendation to buy or sell any investment. Consider an appropriately qualified, authorised professional for advice.
